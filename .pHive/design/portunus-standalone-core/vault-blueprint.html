Vault Interface — Three Plates

Brief

Three information architectures for the standalone Portunus UI

A human needs to see, add, move, and roll secrets — tags and state, never values — without becoming a second privileged path around the broker. Each plate below is a genuinely different shape for that job, not a re-skin: pick the one whose workflow matches how you'll actually use this day to day.

SubjectPortunus Vault, localhost UI v1
AudienceYou — single operator
Jobview · add · rotate, never a value
01

Ask Bar

command-first · leads with portunus ask

One line at the top of the screen, always focused: describe what you need in plain language and the match (or the disambiguation) appears beneath it. Browsing the full registry is a secondary, collapsed view. This is the UI equivalent of the semantic front door — retrieval is the whole point, not a feature bolted onto a table.

Best for — fast, in-the-moment retrieval; leans hardest into the north-star "just ask" workflow.

02

Console

table-first · ops-console density

A filterable table of every reference — provider, project, env, state — with a faceted rail on the left and a detail drawer on the right. Nothing is more than one click away. This is the shape for bulk visibility: scanning everything you hold, spotting a stale state=dropped entry, reviewing the audit trail for one row without losing your place in the rest.

Best for — management at scale, audit review, "what do I have and is it healthy."

03

Vault Map

grouped gallery · browse by provider → project

Cards clustered by provider, then project — a spatial map of what you hold rather than a query or a spreadsheet. Good for skimming ("what do I have for GitHub?") and for a UI that still reads clearly to someone who isn't reaching for exact tag syntax. A middle ground between Ask Bar's precision and Console's density.

Best for — visual browsing, onboarding a second person, "show me the shape of the vault."

Your call

Tell me which plate — or mix elements across them

All three sit on the same backend contract (Broker-gated writes, localhost-only, add-secret is the one human-plaintext-entry point) — the choice only changes the information architecture, not the safety invariants. Reasonable to mix, too: Ask Bar's input as the primary entry point with Console's drawer for detail, for instance.

01 · Ask BarFastest for "get me X now." Weakest for seeing everything at once.
02 · ConsoleBest overview and audit review. Busiest screen, most engineering.
03 · Vault MapMost approachable to skim. Least precise for exact retrieval.
